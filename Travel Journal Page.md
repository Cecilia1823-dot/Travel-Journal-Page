# Travel Journal Page

Use the image-editing tool to turn the user's photograph into one polished portrait journaling sheet. Preserve the source's identity and recognizable content while translating it into a contemporary handmade editorial style.

## Style Definition

The target look combines:

- modern Asian lifestyle-magazine composition;
- handmade scrapbook and travel-journal warmth;
- watercolor and colored-pencil printing;
- layered paper-cut and pop-up-book construction;
- clean die-cut sticker materials;
- soft mint and butter-cream color blocking;
- asymmetrical editorial spacing with strong hierarchy;
- restrained coral-orange and lemon-yellow accents.

The image should feel fresh, tactile, joyful, refined, and memorable—not childish, cluttered, or kitschy.

## Default Composition

Create a tall portrait canvas with two visually distinct areas. Use color fields instead of a drawn divider.

### Upper Hero Area

- Give the upper area about 60–68% of the canvas.
- Use a soft desaturated mint-green paper field with extremely subtle natural grain.
- Preserve the complete source scene inside one dominant cream paper frame with gently irregular, rounded hand-cut corners.
- A 1–2 degree frame tilt is allowed when it adds energy without looking careless.
- Keep the complete scene recognizable: viewpoint, main subject, important surroundings, relative positions, and source-specific details.
- Translate important subjects into layered flat printed paper cutouts. Show cream cut edges, subtle cardstock thickness, small fold tabs or V-fold supports, and soft controlled cast shadows.
- Keep printed surfaces mostly flat. Depth must come from paper layering and attachment, not rounded CGI volume.

### Lower Sticker Area

- Give the lower area about 32–40% of the canvas.
- Use a warm butter-ivory paper field with minimal texture.
- Extract 5–9 of the most distinctive people, animals, objects, foods, accessories, or meaningful details from the source.
- Each sticker must be a complete, isolated subject with a consistent cream-white die-cut border and a very light paper shadow.
- Remove all original environment from inside sticker silhouettes.
- Arrange stickers as a balanced editorial constellation: varied scale, slight natural rotations, intentional negative space, and no rigid equal-sized grid.
- Use one or two larger anchor stickers, several medium stickers, and smaller supporting stickers.
- Do not overlap, crop, duplicate, or touch stickers. Preserve enough space to cut or reuse each item.
- At most one or two small elements may cross the boundary modestly when this creates a deliberate visual bridge and does not obstruct the hero scene.

If the user requests separate files, create the hero scene and sticker sheet separately. Otherwise, create one combined two-area sheet.

## Color and Finish

- Default fields: soft desaturated mint above and warm butter cream below.
- Preserve important source colors so identity and appetizing or emotional qualities remain intact.
- Use coral orange and lemon yellow only as 2–4 small flat-paper accent shapes. These accents must not compete with the source.
- Use warm, gentle light and clean warm/cool contrast.
- Maintain watercolor washes, colored-pencil contouring, soft printed edges, subtle paper fibers, and carefully controlled shadows.
- Keep backgrounds quiet enough that the hero scene and stickers remain dominant.

Follow explicit user palette choices instead of the default palette. Preserve the same hierarchy and paper logic when colors change.

## Texture Integrity

Natural texture is essential, but artificial surface noise is forbidden.

- Render people, animals, objects, and foods with clean watercolor gradients and sparse material-specific cues.
- Use only believable details such as fabric weave, fur direction, wood grain, stone speckle, leaf veins, food marbling, pores, seeds, and soft highlights.
- Keep paper grain subtle, random, low contrast, and non-symbolic.
- Paper folds belong at cutout edges, bases, hinges, and support tabs—not across faces, skin, food, clothing, or product surfaces.

Remove or avoid all meaningless embossed grooves, pseudo-writing, fake letters, glyphs, stamped symbols, repeated squiggles, carved networks, face-like marks, decorative engraving, plastic relief, and glossy molded patterns. If any appear after generation, revise the image with a texture-cleanup edit before delivery.

## Preservation and Boundaries

- Preserve recognizable faces, pose, expression, clothing, markings, silhouettes, products, labels, food identity, and important scene details.
- Do not invent unrelated characters, objects, foods, accessories, logos, or narrative elements.
- Do not convert the entire scene into a photorealistic miniature diorama.
- Do not render subjects as plastic toys, clay figures, plush dolls, rounded CGI models, glossy miniatures, or solid sculptures.
- Avoid harsh neon, dark muddy palettes, beige-on-beige monotony, rigid rows, excessive decorative fragments, grid or ruled backgrounds, prominent divider lines, torn divider strips, captions, dates, labels, text, logos, and watermarks unless the user explicitly requests them.

## Image-Tool Prompt

Use the supplied photo as the edit target. State explicitly:

1. which image is the edit target;
2. that the output is a tall two-area editorial journaling sheet;
3. the upper and lower proportions and their contrasting paper color fields;
4. which complete scene appears in the dominant upper hand-cut frame;
5. which subjects become layered flat paper cutouts and how they attach to the page;
6. which 5–9 source items are isolated as lower die-cut stickers;
7. sticker completeness, borders, spacing, scale variation, and non-overlap;
8. the watercolor, colored-pencil, paper-cut, and warm editorial treatment;
9. the required source invariants;
10. the texture-integrity exclusions, especially no pseudo-writing, engraving, or plastic relief;
11. no text, logo, watermark, unrelated content, rounded CGI, or fully 3D background.

Generate directly when the source and subject are clear. Ask only when the edit target is missing or the intended main subject is genuinely ambiguous.

## Review

Inspect the result when possible and revise if any test fails:

- **Hierarchy:** Is the upper framed scene clearly dominant?
- **Separation:** Do mint and cream fields create two areas without a heavy divider?
- **Pop-up:** Do the chosen subjects read as flat paper cutouts attached to a page?
- **Source fidelity:** Are identity, composition, and important details recognizable?
- **Sticker utility:** Are lower items isolated, complete, bordered, non-overlapping, and reusable?
- **Editorial rhythm:** Do varied scale, asymmetry, and negative space feel intentional?
- **Texture:** Are surfaces natural and free of symbols, engravings, pseudo-writing, and plastic patterns?
- **Mood:** Does the sheet feel fresh, warm, joyful, refined, and visually memorable?

Deliver the result as a pop-up editorial journaling sheet with extracted sticker materials, not as a 3D model.
